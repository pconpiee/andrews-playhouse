Snapshot

  • Founded: 1874. The flagship university of the Seventh-day Adventist Church in North America.
  • Undergraduate enrollment: ~1,500.
  • Faith affiliation: Seventh-day Adventist; Sabbath observance (Friday sundown to Saturday sundown) is central.
  • Nursing program: Direct-entry Bachelor of Science in Nursing, CCNE-accredited.
  • Sticker cost: ~$38,000/year all-in (tuition + room + board).
  • International student community: Large — Andrews historically draws Adventist students from 100+ countries.

Why this is on the priority list

Andrews is one of the most international-friendly Christian universities in the United States, with a structured aid model for non-citizen students and a long history of educating Adventist applicants from Africa. The university publishes specific scholarship tiers for international undergraduates and has a Sabbath-observant culture that many Christian students from East Africa find familiar. Andrews intl aid

Nursing program

  • 4-year direct-entry BSN — you apply to the nursing major as part of your initial application; no separate junior-year nursing application.
  • CCNE-accredited (the standard accreditation for BSN programs).
  • Clinical rotations across Berrien County hospitals and AdventHealth network sites.
  • Program emphasizes whole-person care, a hallmark of Adventist health philosophy.

Cost reality

Sticker price is ~$38,000/year all-in. With the Andrews Distinguished International Scholarship and Adventist denominational aid, students with strong academics often pay $18,000–$24,000/year. That's still significant for an Ethiopian family, but it's among the lowest realistic out-of-pocket numbers for direct-entry BSN study in the US.

  • Year 1 funding required for I-20: roughly the cost-after-scholarship.
  • On-campus work: Andrews offers extensive on-campus student employment; F-1 students are limited to 20 hours/week during the school year.

International aid story

  • The Andrews Distinguished International Scholarship is tiered by academic record and is renewable based on GPA. scholarship tiers
  • Adventist students may qualify for additional denominational aid from their home conference.
  • The Lake Union Conference (the regional Adventist body) sometimes funds students from missionary or Adventist Church families.
  • Need-based aid for international students is limited; merit is the primary lever.

City vibe + climate

Berrien Springs is a small, quiet college town in rural southwestern Michigan, along the St. Joseph River. It's about 100 miles east of Chicago, but you won't go to Chicago every weekend — most life is on-campus or in nearby Niles.

Climate vs. Addis: Major adjustment. Addis averages ~16°C year-round; Berrien Springs winters drop below freezing for months, with significant snow. Summers are warm and humid. You will need real winter clothing — a proper coat, boots, layers — most of which is much cheaper to buy in the US than to bring.

Church life

Adventist-majority campus. The Pioneer Memorial Church on campus is the largest Adventist congregation in the area and the spiritual center of student life. Sabbath services (Saturday morning) are central — most students attend, and there's a strong tradition of Friday-night vespers.

For students from other Christian denominations, Berrien County has Baptist, Methodist, Catholic, and non-denominational churches within a 20-minute drive. The Ethiopian Adventist community is small in southwest Michigan; the larger East African Adventist diaspora is concentrated in the Chicago area (90 minutes west).

Activities + work-life balance

Quiet, observant culture — Sabbath sets aside Friday sundown to Saturday sundown, which means no studying or work assigned during that window. Many students find that rhythm protective during a heavy program like nursing.

Active intramural sports, music ensembles, and outdoor recreation (lake, river, hiking). International Club is well-established and a useful social anchor for new arrivals.

Post-graduation

  • NCLEX-RN: US-accredited BSN graduates sit the NCLEX directly. NCSBN
  • OPT: 12-month standard OPT (nursing is not on STEM-OPT). Most graduates use this year to start work at an Adventist Health hospital. USCIS OPT
  • Employer pipeline: AdventHealth (a 50-hospital US network), Loma Linda University Health, and other Adventist systems actively recruit Andrews nursing graduates.

Ask admissions

Email these to nursing-admissions before applying:

  • What is the typical international student aid package for the BSN program, after the Distinguished Scholarship?
  • Are Sabbath accommodations standard during clinicals and hospital rotations?
  • Is there an Ethiopian Student Association or East African community on campus?
  • What percentage of international BSN graduates secure US nursing employment within 6 months of graduation?
  • Can the Distinguished Scholarship stack with denominational aid from my home Adventist conference?

Application deadline: rolling. Apply early — fall semester aid is awarded as applications come in.
