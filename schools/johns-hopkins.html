Johns Hopkins University

Baltimore, Maryland · Private research university · Graduate-entry nursing (no direct BSN) · ~$65k/yr sticker · Intl aid score 3/5

Important: no direct-entry BSN here

Johns Hopkins School of Nursing is graduate-entry. To become a nurse via Hopkins, you'd do an undergraduate degree (anywhere) and then apply to Hopkins' MSN-Entry into Nursing program. Hopkins is on this list as a long-shot reach: the undergraduate university is need-aware for international applicants (ability to pay influences admission), but Hopkins meets 100% of demonstrated need for international students it admits. Snapshot

  • Founded: 1876 — America's first research university.
  • Undergraduate enrollment: ~6,300.
  • Faith affiliation: None.
  • Nursing program structure: Graduate-entry only via the Johns Hopkins School of Nursing.
  • Hospital: Johns Hopkins Hospital — one of the top-ranked hospitals in the world.
  • Sticker cost (undergrad): ~$85,000/year all-in. Need-aware for international applicants (meets full need for those admitted).
  • Sticker cost (Master's Entry into Nursing): ~$65,000/year.

Why this is on the priority list

Johns Hopkins is on this list as a long-shot, high-leverage path. The university is need-AWARE for international applicants — meaning your demonstrated financial need can lower your admit odds — but if admitted, Hopkins meets 100% of demonstrated need for international students. After graduating, the Master's Entry into Nursing program at Hopkins is one of the strongest pathways into US nursing in the world, with a direct pipeline to Johns Hopkins Hospital. The road is longer (5–6 years to RN instead of 4). JHU Nursing intl

The two paths

Path 1 — Undergraduate then MSN-Entry

  • Apply to Johns Hopkins University undergraduate (need-AWARE for international applicants, but meets full need for those admitted).
  • Major in a pre-nursing-relevant field (biology, public health, neuroscience).
  • Apply to the MSN-Entry into Nursing program after graduation. Two-year program.
  • Total time: 6 years. Total cost-after-aid: potentially lowest of any path here if undergrad aid is generous.
  • End credential: MSN + NCLEX-RN eligibility.

Path 2 — MSN-Entry direct (after BSN-equivalent elsewhere)

  • Earn an undergraduate degree elsewhere (any field).
  • Apply directly to Hopkins MSN-Entry as a graduate applicant.
  • Two-year program; graduate-level scholarships and assistantships are how funding works.
  • Total time depends on undergrad path. Cost-after-aid is harder to predict.

Cost reality

Undergraduate sticker is ~$85,000/year — eye-watering. Hopkins is need-aware for international undergraduates (so applying for aid lowers your admit odds), but Hopkins meets 100% of demonstrated need for international students it admits. If you're admitted with full need, out-of-pocket can be near zero. The graduate MSN program is also need-aware; graduate aid is more limited than undergraduate aid.

International aid story

  • Undergrad: Need-aware for international applicants — ability to pay influences admission decisions. Meets 100% of demonstrated need for those admitted, with grants (no loans). JHU intl applicants   JHU financial aid
  • Graduate MSN: Scholarships and graduate assistantships available; cost-after-aid still substantial.
  • Both paths require strong academics — Hopkins admit rate is ~6%.

City vibe + climate

The Homewood undergraduate campus is in a historic, leafy neighborhood of north Baltimore. The School of Nursing and the medical campus are in East Baltimore, a 10-minute shuttle ride away. Baltimore is a mid-sized city with deep history, world-class hospitals, and significant economic inequality — students have access to extensive community-health and urban-clinical experiences.

Climate vs. Addis: Four real seasons. Winters cold (often below freezing) but milder than Chicago or Boston. Summers humid. Beautiful cherry-blossom spring.

Church life

Diverse religious life on campus and in Baltimore. Strong Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox communities. Baltimore has a substantial Ethiopian community, with at least two Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo churches in the metro area and Ethiopian restaurants in several neighborhoods. The Hopkins chaplaincy supports a wide range of student religious groups.

Activities + work-life balance

Intense academic culture — Hopkins is famous for it. Demanding, fast-paced, but also generously resourced. Pre-nursing or pre-health students typically take a heavy science load.

Post-graduation

  • If you complete the MSN-Entry path: NCLEX-RN eligibility plus a master's degree — a strong credential anywhere.
  • Direct pipeline to Johns Hopkins Hospital (consistently ranked among the top 5 hospitals in the US).
  • Standard 12-month OPT after MSN graduation.
  • An MSN sets you up for advanced practice roles (nurse practitioner, clinical nurse leader) faster than a BSN-only path.

Ask admissions

  • If admitted to JHU undergraduate need-blind, what's the typical net cost for a family with limited US-dollar income?
  • What undergraduate majors best prepare students for the MSN-Entry program?
  • How competitive is direct admission to MSN-Entry from JHU undergraduate vs. external?
  • What is the NCLEX-RN first-attempt pass rate for MSN-Entry graduates?
  • Are there graduate aid opportunities (assistantships, scholarships) specifically for MSN-Entry?

Application deadlines: Undergraduate Regular Decision Jan 2; Early Decision Nov 1. MSN-Entry deadlines on the School of Nursing site.
