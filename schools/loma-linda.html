Loma Linda University

Snapshot

  • Founded: 1905. A health-sciences university built around the Loma Linda University Medical Center.
  • Undergraduate enrollment: ~1,200 (graduate-heavy university overall).
  • Faith affiliation: Seventh-day Adventist; the entire town of Loma Linda is one of the world's "Blue Zones" — communities with exceptional longevity attributed in part to Adventist health practices.
  • Nursing program: Pre-licensure BSN, CCNE-accredited. NCLEX pass rates historically ~95%. LLU Nursing
  • Sticker cost: ~$42,000/year all-in.

Why this is on the priority list

Loma Linda's nursing program feeds directly into one of the largest Adventist hospital systems in the United States. The university's mission — "to make man whole" — is integrated into the curriculum. For Adventist students or Christian students drawn to a service-oriented healthcare community, this is one of the strongest direct-entry BSN environments in the country.

Nursing program

  • Bachelor of Science in Nursing, CCNE-accredited.
  • Clinical rotations primarily at Loma Linda University Medical Center, a Level 1 trauma center and one of the most active children's hospitals in California.
  • Strong simulation lab and human-patient simulator program.
  • Adventist health philosophy integrated throughout — whole-person care, plant-based nutrition modules, faith-and-health framing.

Cost reality

Sticker is ~$42,000/year. International undergraduate aid is more limited than at Andrews — Loma Linda's institutional aid budget skews graduate. Realistic out-of-pocket for an international BSN student: $30,000–$38,000/year, before any external scholarships.

The compensating factor: students often work at Loma Linda University Medical Center while studying (on-campus employment counts under F-1 rules), which both offsets cost and builds a direct relationship with the eventual employer.

International aid story

  • Some merit scholarships are available to international undergraduates; eligibility is competitive.
  • Adventist denominational aid from your home conference can supplement.
  • External nursing scholarships (Bold.org, AACN) are worth pursuing in parallel.
  • If the cost-after-aid still requires a loan to close the gap, this is not the right school. Loans at 12–15% interest can swallow your first decade of nursing income — walk away rather than borrow.

City vibe + climate

Loma Linda is a small, health-focused town in the Inland Empire region of Southern California, about 60 miles east of Los Angeles. The town is built around the hospital — most residents work in healthcare, and Adventist culture pervades everyday life.

Climate vs. Addis: Hot, dry summers (often 35°C+) and mild winters (rarely below 10°C). No snow. Easier on the body than Midwest or Northeast options. Smog and air quality can be an issue in summer.

Church life

The Loma Linda University Church seats 6,000 and is the spiritual hub of campus and town. Sabbath observance is universal — most local businesses close Friday sundown to Saturday sundown.

The broader Southern California Adventist community is large and diverse, with active Ethiopian and East African Adventist congregations in the greater Los Angeles area (~60–90 minute drive).

Activities + work-life balance

Service-oriented culture; the university's tagline drives student life. Many students participate in medical mission trips during summers and breaks. On-campus recreation is solid; the proximity to mountains (San Bernardino National Forest) and beaches (90 min) is a real perk.

The program is academically demanding — expect 25–30 hours/week of study on top of clinicals. Sabbath provides a built-in rest day.

Post-graduation

  • NCLEX-RN pass rates ~95% — among the highest in California.
  • Loma Linda University Health hires aggressively from its own nursing graduates.
  • Adventist Health, Kaiser Permanente, and Cedars-Sinai also recruit on campus.
  • Standard 12-month OPT after graduation (nursing is not STEM-OPT eligible).
  • California is one of the highest-paying states for new nurses in the US.

Ask admissions

  • What is the typical Year-1 financial-aid package for international BSN applicants?
  • Are there on-campus employment opportunities at LLU Medical Center for first-year nursing students on F-1?
  • How does the program support international students through NCLEX prep and California licensure?
  • What is the average time-to-employment for international BSN graduates?
  • Are there housing options that are walkable to campus for students who don't yet have a US driver's license?

Application deadline: February 1 typical for fall entry.
