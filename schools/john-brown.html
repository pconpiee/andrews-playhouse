John Brown University

Snapshot

  • Founded: 1919. Interdenominational evangelical Christian university.
  • Undergraduate enrollment: ~1,500.
  • Faith affiliation: Interdenominational; emphasizes Christ-centered education without a single denominational affiliation.
  • Nursing program: Traditional 4-year BSN, CCNE-accredited; NCLEX-RN pass rate historically ~96% on first attempt.
  • Sticker cost: ~$30,000/year all-in — among the lowest of priority Lane B schools.
  • International + missionary-kid community: Unusually large for a US university of this size.

Why this is on the priority list

JBU is one of the most consistent practical winners on cost-after-aid for international Christian applicants from the developing world. The university has long supported missionary-kid (MK) families and Christian families from East and West Africa, and its merit aid for international students is generous relative to the sticker price. NCLEX outcomes and CCNE accreditation are solid. JBU Nursing

Nursing program

  • Traditional 4-year BSN with direct admission to the nursing major.
  • CCNE-accredited.
  • NCLEX-RN first-attempt pass rate historically ~96%, well above the national average.
  • Clinical rotations in Northwest Arkansas hospital systems (Mercy, Washington Regional, Children's Northwest).
  • Faith-and-vocation integration in the curriculum.

Cost reality

Sticker is ~$30,000/year. With JBU's merit aid for international Christian students, realistic out-of-pocket can come down to $15,000–$22,000/year. That makes JBU one of the most affordable direct-entry BSN options on this list when you net everything out.

Cost of living in Siloam Springs is among the lowest of any school on this list — groceries, off-campus housing, and utilities are notably cheaper than the coasts.

International aid story

  • JBU advertises significant merit scholarships for international students; tiered by academic record.
  • Specific aid streams for missionary-kids (MK) and pastors' kids (PK) — even if your father runs a non-profit serving a Christian community, this can be worth raising with the admissions office.
  • Active partnerships with Christian organizations that fund students from developing-world contexts.
  • JBU is unusually friendly in considering family financial documentation that doesn't fit standard US templates.

JBU intl admissions

City vibe + climate

Siloam Springs is a small town in the Arkansas Ozarks, near the Oklahoma border, about 30 minutes from Fayetteville (home of the University of Arkansas) and 90 minutes from Tulsa, Oklahoma. The town is safe, affordable, walkable, and slow-paced — closer to a small American college town than a city.

Climate vs. Addis: Hot summers (often 32°C+) and mild winters with some snow. Spring is glorious, autumn is long. No tropical extremes. Tornado season runs spring-to-early-summer.

Church life

Interdenominational evangelical campus culture; chapel three times per week. Small groups, worship gatherings, and student ministry teams are the spine of campus life. Many students come from Christian families and international missions backgrounds, so the community feels familiar to students from Christian communities in Africa.

Siloam Springs has Baptist, Methodist, non-denominational, and Catholic churches within walking distance. There is a small Ethiopian community in the broader Northwest Arkansas region.

Activities + work-life balance

Tight-knit, supportive campus — the dean of students and faculty are accessible. Active intramural sports, outdoor recreation (the Ozarks are beautiful), and a strong honors program.

Lower cost of living plus smaller class sizes mean BSN students often have time to work part-time on campus during the school year.

Post-graduation

  • NCLEX-RN eligibility from this CCNE-accredited program.
  • Most graduates work in Arkansas, Oklahoma, Texas, or Missouri healthcare systems initially.
  • Mercy Health, Washington Regional, and Children's Northwest hospitals all hire JBU graduates.
  • Standard 12-month OPT after graduation.

Ask admissions

  • What is the typical international merit-aid award after the standard merit scholarship?
  • Are there specific aid streams for students from Christian missions or non-profit families?
  • What is the realistic Year-1 out-of-pocket cost for an admitted international BSN student with an 85% high-school average and a TOEFL of 100?
  • How does JBU support the NCLEX-RN preparation process for international students?
  • What does the international student community look like — countries represented, student associations?

Application deadline: March 1 typical for fall entry; rolling admission available with priority for early applicants.
