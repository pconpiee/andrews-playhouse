Glossary

Every acronym, spelled out.

If you've seen a term on this site you don't recognise, it's here. Print this page or keep it open in a second tab while you work.

Tests & applications

TOEFL iBT
Test of English as a Foreign Language, Internet-Based Test. The English-language proficiency exam most US universities require from international applicants whose first language is not English. Costs ~$200 per attempt. Scored 0–120.
IELTS
International English Language Testing System. An alternative to TOEFL accepted by most US universities.
SAT
Scholastic Assessment Test. A standardised US college admissions test scored 400–1600. Many schools are now "test-optional" but a strong score widens scholarship eligibility.
ACT
American College Testing. The other US college admissions exam, scored 1–36. Schools typically accept either SAT or ACT.
Common App
The Common Application — one online application accepted by 1,000+ US colleges. Opens August 1 each year for the following Fall admit cycle.
NursingCAS
The Nursing Centralized Application Service. A separate application platform used by some BSN programs in addition to or instead of Common App.
CSS Profile
The College Scholarship Service Profile. A detailed financial-aid application administered by the College Board, required by most need-blind elite schools.
ISFAA
International Student Financial Aid Application. Used by some US schools to assess intl student financial need.
ED
Early Decision. A binding application option — if admitted, you must attend. Available at many private universities. Deadline typically Nov 1.
EA
Early Action. A non-binding early application option. You get an admission decision earlier but are not required to commit.
WES / ECE / SpanTran
Credential evaluation services that translate non-US school records into a US-comparable format. Required by some universities for international applicants. Cost ~$160–$300, takes 4–6 weeks.

Nursing degrees & licensure

BSN
Bachelor of Science in Nursing — the standard 4-year US undergraduate nursing degree. The recommended path for international students because it qualifies you directly for the NCLEX-RN.
ADN / ASN
Associate Degree in Nursing (also Associate of Science in Nursing). A 2-year nursing degree typically offered at community colleges. Qualifies you for the NCLEX-RN but limits your career ceiling and scholarship eligibility.
RN
Registered Nurse. A licensed nurse in the United States. To become an RN, you must (a) graduate from an accredited nursing program (BSN or ADN), (b) pass the NCLEX-RN, and (c) obtain a license from a state Board of Nursing.
RN-to-BSN
An online bridge program for already-licensed RNs to earn a BSN. NOT an entry-level path — it requires you to be a licensed RN first.
MSN
Master of Science in Nursing. A graduate nursing degree, typically 2–3 years after a BSN.
MEPN
Master's Entry Program in Nursing. A graduate-entry program for students who have a bachelor's in another field. Typically 2 years. Used by elite universities (Yale, Johns Hopkins, others) that don't offer a direct BSN.
DNP
Doctor of Nursing Practice. The terminal practice-focused nursing doctorate.
NCLEX-RN
National Council Licensure Examination for Registered Nurses. The single US licensure exam every RN must pass to practice. Administered by NCSBN.
NCSBN
National Council of State Boards of Nursing. The body that administers the NCLEX.
BON
Board of Nursing. Each US state has one; it licenses nurses in that state and sets state-specific rules. Some states have rules that limit licensure for F-1 students (e.g. requiring a Social Security Number).
CCNE
Commission on Collegiate Nursing Education. One of the two main accreditation bodies for US BSN programs. CCNE-accredited programs are recognised in all 50 states.
ACEN
Accreditation Commission for Education in Nursing. The other main US nursing-program accreditor.
CGFNS
Commission on Graduates of Foreign Nursing Schools. Provides certification for nurses trained outside the US. If you graduate from a US-accredited program you skip this.
CGFNS VisaScreen
A separate CGFNS product required by US immigration for nurses applying for H-1B or employment-based green cards — needed even by US-trained F-1 graduates.

Visa, immigration & work authorization

F-1
The US visa for full-time academic study. The standard visa for international undergraduates.
J-1
A separate US student/exchange visitor visa, with somewhat different rules and post-study options.
I-20
The Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status. A school issues this once you've documented Year-1 funds. You need it to apply for the F-1 visa.
DSO
Designated School Official. The person at each US school authorised to issue the I-20 and update your immigration record.
SEVIS
Student and Exchange Visitor Information System. The US database tracking all international students. The SEVIS I-901 fee ($350) is paid before your visa interview.
DS-160
The online Nonimmigrant Visa Application. You complete this before your F-1 visa interview at the US embassy.
MRV
Machine Readable Visa fee. The application fee paid to the US embassy ($185 as of 2025; confirm before paying).
OPT
Optional Practical Training. 12 months of US work authorization after an F-1 student graduates, in their field of study.
STEM-OPT
A 24-month extension to standard OPT for STEM-major graduates. Traditional BSN does not qualify.
H-1B
A US specialty-occupation work visa, lottery-based, 3-year renewable. Harder to get for nurses than for software engineers.
Schedule A
A US Department of Labor list of occupations with a pre-certified labor shortage. Registered nurses are on Schedule A, which lets employers sponsor a green card faster (skipping the PERM labor-certification step).
EB-3
Employment-Based Third Preference green card. The standard employer-sponsored permanent-residence path for nurses, made faster by Schedule A.
I-134 / I-864
Affidavit of Support forms. I-134 is used for the F-1 visa (less binding); I-864 is used for green cards (legally binding for years). When a relative signs either, they enter a financial commitment to you.
SSN
Social Security Number. A US tax identification number required for many things including state nursing licensure in several states. F-1 students can obtain one only through on-campus employment.

Money & aid

FAFSA
Free Application for Federal Student Aid. The US federal financial aid form. Closed to non-citizens.
Pell Grant
The largest US federal need-based grant. US citizens only.
HRSA Nurse Corps
Health Resources and Services Administration. Runs the Nurse Corps Scholarship Program, which pays full tuition + a living stipend in exchange for a work commitment at an underserved-area facility. US citizens/permanent residents only.
Need-blind admission
A school admits students without considering their ability to pay. Only a handful of US schools are need-blind for international applicants.
Need-aware admission
A school considers ability to pay when admitting international applicants. The default at most US schools.
Full need / meets full need
A school that, once it admits you, covers 100% of your demonstrated financial need with grants and/or work-study (not necessarily loans). All need-blind elites also meet full need.
Demonstrated need
The difference between a school's cost of attendance and your family's expected contribution, as calculated by the CSS Profile or each school's intl aid form.
Sticker price
The published total cost (tuition + fees + room + board) before any aid. Most international students pay something less than sticker.
Merit aid
Scholarships awarded for academic record, not financial need. Open to many international applicants.
Institutional aid
Aid from the university itself (as opposed to government or private). The biggest lever for international students.

Programs & resources

EducationUSA
A US Department of State network of advising centres in 175+ countries. Free, authoritative. The Addis Ababa branch is the single best resource for Ethiopian applicants.
ESP
EducationUSA Scholars Program. A selective preparatory programme for Grade 11 Ethiopian students. Past Grade 11? Book general EducationUSA advising instead.
AACN
American Association of Colleges of Nursing. Maintains a scholarship hub and accreditation standards.
NCF
Nurses Christian Fellowship. Maintains a directory of Christian nursing schools and faith-based scholarships.
MMI
Multiple Mini Interview. An interview format used by many nursing schools — 6–10 short stations (~8 minutes each) with different evaluators. More objective than a single panel interview.
AAUW
American Association of University Women. Sponsors international fellowships for women pursuing graduate study in the US.
MMEG
Margaret McNamara Education Grants. Funds women from developing countries pursuing US graduate study.
