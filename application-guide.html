The whole road, in six phases.

Each phase has a checklist that saves in your browser. Don't try to do it all at once — work the phase you're in and trust the next phase will come when it's time.

Why a 14-month gap?

You graduate in June 2026. The earliest realistic US start date is August 2027 — because the US application cycle for the autumn semester opens in August of the previous year (Common Application opens August 1, 2026), with most deadlines in November–January. That means the 14 months between graduation and arrival are not lost time — they are the application year. Phase 0 starts immediately.

Earn your test scores, build the shortlist, draft the essay.

Phase 2 — Fall 2026 (applications)

Submit. Aim to be done by January 1 even for Regular Decision schools — early submissions get more attention from admissions officers.

Phase 3 — Winter 2026/27 (interviews + aid)

Some schools interview; some don't. If they do, expect a Multiple Mini Interview (MMI) for nursing.

Phase 4 — Spring 2027 (I-20 + F-1 visa)

The legal and logistical phase. Schools won't issue an I-20 (the certificate of eligibility you need for the visa) until you've documented proof of funds. The full visa briefing lives on the Visa & Licensure page.

Phase 5 — Summer 2027 (logistics + arrival)

You can enter the US up to 30 days before your I-20 program start date.

Three things that catch people out

F-1 needs proof of funds per year.

You don't need to prove four years upfront — only Year 1. But you do need a credible plan for Year 2+ (scholarship renewal, family support, sponsor commitment).

Nursing isn't on the STEM-OPT list.

Standard 12-month OPT after graduation only; no 24-month STEM extension. Plan for an H-1B, employer sponsorship, or further graduate study to stay long-term.

CGFNS vs. NCLEX.

CGFNS certification is for foreign-trained nurses seeking US licensure. If you earn your BSN at a US-accredited school, you skip CGFNS and go straight to NCLEX-RN. CGFNS
